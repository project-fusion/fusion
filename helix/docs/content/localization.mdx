# Localization and RTL

## Right-to-left already works

Set `dir` and the layout flips:

```html
<html
  dir="rtl"
  lang="ar"
>
```

Every component is written in logical properties, `inline-start` rather than
`left`, `margin-block` rather than `margin-top`. Nothing infers the direction
for you and nothing needs to: the document says it and the components follow.

**A physical side appears only where the thing it places is physical.** A
floating panel is placed from coordinates measured from the viewport's left
edge, so it and its arrow are written in `left` and `top`. Everything a reader
would expect to mirror still does.

**Charts mirror too.** A category or time axis starts where the text does, so
on a right-to-left page the first reading is on the right and the drawing,
its labels, its hover targets and its tooltips all turn together. Values keep
their vertical sense and numerals are not reversed. A whole donut stays
clockwise from twelve, which is a clock's convention rather than a script's,
and a scatter chart keeps its left-to-right plane, both of its axes being
measures. [Charting](./charting) has the rest.

**Carets and arrow keys turn too.** A caret meaning next or previous, the one
between two crumbs and a shut disclosure all point along the line, so on a
right-to-left page they point the other way, and every arrow key moves where it
points on screen: the left arrow moves to the next tab, the next day, the next
control in a toolbar, and opens a tree's branch or a menu's submenu, whose caret
points left. A slider and a splitter's divider move the way the key points.

A media player is the exception: its timeline and its skip buttons run left to
right in every script, a tape having one direction, so the left arrow rewinds.

That is also how `placement` reads. The side is physical and the alignment is
logical: `right` is the right of the anchor in either direction, and
`bottom-start` lines a panel up with the anchor's start edge, which is its right
edge on a right-to-left page. A panel with no room on its side still flips to
the other one, so a `right-start` panel beside a rail at the start of a
right-to-left page opens to its left.

The same is true of anything you write on top. Use logical properties and your
layout flips with ours; use `left` and `right` and it will not.

## Locale-aware controls

Six families format numbers or dates and take a `locale`:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Date Input`, `Date Picker` | Date formats and first day of week |
| `Time Input`, `Time Picker` | 12 or 24 hour, and separators |
| `Pagination` | Grouped thousands in page numbers |
| `File Input` | File sizes |

```html
<helix-pagination
  locale="de-DE"
  pages="1200"
></helix-pagination>
```

Left unset, they use the browser's own locale, which is usually right. Set it
when your application's language is a choice the reader made rather than the one
their operating system is in.

Formatting goes through `Intl`, so there is no locale data bundled and nothing
to keep up to date.

## Translatable strings

Seventy properties across the headless tier are strings a reader sees, and every
one is a property rather than a hardcoded word. The ones that combine
values are format templates:

```html
<helix-pagination-summary
  format="{from} bis {to} von {total}"
  locale="de-DE"
  page="2"
  page-size="10"
  total="1500"
></helix-pagination-summary>
```

The placeholders are named rather than positional, so a translation can reorder
them, which some languages require and a positional format cannot express.

**Pass these from your own translation system.** Helix ships English defaults
and no translation machinery: a design system that owned the string catalog
would be a second one to keep in step with yours.

## What to check when adding a language

- **Long strings.** German and Finnish will find every control that assumed a
  short label. Controls take their width from the layout rather than carrying
  one, which helps, but a toolbar that fitted six buttons may now fit four.
- **The `lang` attribute, not just the strings.** Hyphenation, quotation marks
  and font fallback all key off it.
- **Numerals.** `Intl` handles the digits; a hardcoded `%` or a currency symbol
  in your own markup will not move to the right side.
- **Icons that point.** A caret a component draws turns on its own. An icon you
  place does not unless you ask, because the names are physical: `mirror` turns
  one on a right-to-left page, for an arrow that means forward or back.

  ```html
  <helix-button>
    Continue
    <helix-icon
      mirror
      name="arrow-right"
      slot="end"
    ></helix-icon>
  </helix-button>
  ```

## What is not covered

There is no message catalog, no pluralization helper and no date-range
formatter beyond what the controls do for themselves. Those belong to your
application's i18n layer, and every one of these properties is designed to be
fed from it.
