# Color

Colors are the largest group of the library's tokens, and they make up most of
a theme. Each color token is named for its role. No token is named for how it
looks, because a name like `--brand-blue-500` becomes wrong as soon as you change
the theme.

## Color roles

### Grounds

Grounds are the colors that content sits on. `background` is the page itself.
The surfaces above it form an ordered ladder:

[//]: # (tokens:surface-ladder)

| Token | Sample | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--helix-color-background` | <span data-token-swatch style={{ background: 'var(--helix-color-background)' }} /> | The page background. Every other surface sits on it. |
| `--helix-color-surface` | <span data-token-swatch style={{ background: 'var(--helix-color-surface)' }} /> | A surface on the page, such as a card or a panel. The separation policy sets it apart with a fill, a border, or a shadow. |
| `--helix-color-surface-raised` | <span data-token-swatch style={{ background: 'var(--helix-color-surface-raised)' }} /> | A panel raised inside a surface, such as a card inside a card. Its fill is one step down the ramp. |
| `--helix-color-surface-overlay` | <span data-token-swatch style={{ background: 'var(--helix-color-surface-overlay)' }} /> | A surface over the page, such as a menu, a popover, or a dialog. Its edge and its shadow set it apart. |
| `--helix-color-surface-sunken` | <span data-token-swatch style={{ background: 'var(--helix-color-surface-sunken)' }} /> | A recessed surface, such as a track or a groove. Its fill sets it apart. A field reads `well`. |

[//]: # (/tokens)

Choose a surface by where the element sits relative to the page. This is the
choice people most often get wrong. See [Surfaces and
separation](./surfaces-and-separation).

A field is filled with `well`: 5% of `foreground` laid over whatever the field
sits on, so its color depends on the surface under the field. `surface-filled`
is a wash of `foreground` too, for a surface with `separation="filled"`, such as
a filled Card or the ground of a spatial chart: 5% in light and 8.5% in dark.
Inside a Dialog, a Drawer, a Popover, an elevated Card, or a sunken region, the
dark wash is 6%, so a filled Card there stays under an elevated one. A row under the
pointer or the keyboard, such as an option, is lit with `hover` the same way:
8% of `foreground` in light and 12% in dark.

A Button washes with its own tone and leaves `hover` alone. Each emphasis takes
its own steps:

| Emphasis | At rest | Under the pointer | Under a press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ghost` | None | 8% in light, 12% in dark | 16% in light, 20% in dark |
| `outlined` | None | 12% | 16% in light, 20% in dark |
| `subtle` | 14% | 22% | 30% |

An Icon Button takes the same washes.

Four more washes are laid over a ground the same way:

- `press` lights a control under a press, a step past `hover`: 16% of
  `foreground` in light and 19% in dark. A Button's own press is 20% in dark,
  as the table above gives.
- `selected` marks a chosen thing that stays chosen and has no fill of its own,
  such as a range of days or a pressed toggle in a find bar. It is 13% of
  `foreground` in light and 16% in dark.
- `primary-wash` is the bubble of your own message in Chat. It is 20% of
  `primary` with a brand color, and 16% of `foreground` with none.
- `track` is the unfilled length of a bar, such as a Progress or Meter track.
  It is 8% of `foreground` in light and 7% in dark, which on the page is the
  `surface-sunken` rung, and it steps the same distance off a card or a panel.

Three more grounds sit outside the ladder:

- `surface-inverse` is the mode's opposite, for a surface that has to read as
  separate from the page, such as a tooltip. `foreground-inverse` is the text on
  it.
- `surface-media` and `foreground-media` are black and white in both modes. They
  are the well behind a video and the controls drawn over it.
- `overlay` is the scrim behind a modal Dialog, a Drawer, or a Lightbox: a
  near-black at 40% in light mode, and black at 64% in dark.

### Inks

Inks are the colors that go on grounds. The base ink is `foreground`.

- `foreground-muted` is for text that recedes and is still read, such as a
  placeholder, a hint, or a caption. The engine solves it to the contrast
  target.
- `foreground-subtle` recedes further. It is for marks that are not text, such
  as a separator, a chevron, or a grip. It is a fixed step of the gray ramp,
  and the engine does not solve it, so its contrast is not guaranteed. Use
  `foreground-muted` for text that is read. A disabled control fades whole,
  through `--helix-opacity-disabled`.
- Three opacities fade ink by a fixed amount:
  - `--helix-opacity-disabled` is 0.5, for a disabled control.
  - `--helix-opacity-busy` is 0.5, for rows that stay on screen while a new set
    loads.
  - `--helix-opacity-quiet` is 0.72, for quiet text on a ground the text does
    not know, where `foreground-muted` would be the page's gray. A chart
    tooltip's names on the inverse bubble and a message still sending take it.
- Text on a filled color takes the ink named for that fill:
  - `foreground-on-primary` on `primary-surface`
  - `foreground-on-accent` on `accent-surface`
  - `foreground-on-status` on the four status `-surface` fills
  - `background` on `warning-solid`

### Lines and focus

- `border` is the hairline edge of a card, a field, a divider, or a segmented
  control's groove.
- `border-floating` is the edge of a surface that floats over content, such as a
  menu or a popover. It is `border` in light mode and 10% of white in dark mode,
  so the edge still shows where a shadow cannot.
- `border-readonly` is the edge of a read-only mark that carries a value, such
  as a read-only Checkbox's box, a Radio's circle, a Switch's track, a Slider's
  thumb, or a Rating's empty stars. It is 44% of `foreground` in light and 36%
  in dark, from 3.1:1 to 3.4:1 on every ground, so the value still reads where
  the field edge is 1.23:1.
- `border-hover` is the edge of a field under the pointer. It is the brand color
  when one is set, and a gray at about 3:1 on the white page when none is.
- `focus-ring` is the color of the focus ring. It is `primary`, held at a
  lightness of 0.6 or less in light mode and 0.65 or more in dark mode, so the
  ring stays visible on the page.

### Intent

Intent colors show what something means.

- `primary` and `accent` are for emphasis. Each has `-hover`, `-active`, and
  `-subtle` variants. With no brand color set, both are ink: near-black in
  light mode and near-white in dark mode. `primary-surface` and
  `accent-surface` are their fills, each with a `-surface-hover` variant for
  the fill under the pointer and a `-surface-active` variant for the fill under
  a press.
- `info`, `success`, `warning`, and `danger` are for status. Each has a
  `-surface` variant, a filled ground that carries `foreground-on-status`, with
  the same `-surface-hover` and `-surface-active` variants. A solid info,
  success, or danger Button, Tag, Badge, or Icon Button fills its `-surface`
  variant. A solid warning one fills `warning-solid`.
- `primary-strong` is the far end of a ramp drawn in the brand color, such as
  the busiest cell of a heatmap. It is `primary-active` held at a lightness of
  0.33 or more, so the top of the ramp never reads as black.
- `neutral-subtle` is a quiet gray for a mark that stands on the page, such as
  an Avatar with no picture or a neutral Badge. It is 11% of `foreground` over
  the page, mixed in sRGB, and opaque, so one avatar can overlap the next.
- `neutral-surface-hover` and `neutral-surface-active` are a neutral fill under
  the pointer and under a press. The neutral fill is `foreground`, which has
  nothing past it, so both move toward the page, and the press moves further.
- Each of the six tones has a `-muted` variant, such as `danger-muted`: the
  tone's quiet ink, for text with that tone and `emphasis="muted"`. It is the
  tone's color at half the chroma. With no brand color set, `primary-muted` and
  `accent-muted` are `foreground-muted`.
- `warning-mark` is the warning color for a mark, such as a star, a Meter's
  fill, or a Callout's icon. `warning` is solved for text and reads brown as a
  shape, so in light mode a mark takes a lighter, more saturated step that
  measures at least 3:1 on the page. In dark mode it is `warning`.
- `warning-solid` is the warning fill under a label: a solid warning Button,
  Tag, Badge, or Icon Button, and a warning Map Marker or Map Points. Its label
  is `background`. In light mode it is a deep amber, #985f00, under white at
  5.29:1. In dark mode it is `warning-mark`, #ffaf00, under black at 11.38:1.
- `brand-mark` is the brand color for a graphic. The engine solves `primary` to
  the theme's contrast target for text, and solves `brand-mark` to the 3:1 a
  graphic needs, so `brand-mark` stays closer to the color you chose. With no
  brand color set, it matches `primary`.

### Rise and fall

`rise` and `fall` color a value by which way it moved, such as a price. They
are `success` and `danger` unless you set them. Mainland China, Taiwan, and
Japan draw a rise in red, so a page for one of those markets swaps the two. See
[Rise and fall colors](./localization#rise-and-fall-colors).

### Maps

`contour` colors the contour lines of a map, a brown taken 45% of the way toward
`foreground`, so the lines read on a light map and on a dark one.

### Series

Helix has twenty-four series colors for charts. They are ordered, so a chart
with three lines uses the first three. We chose the colors so that the first
ones in the list stay easy to tell apart, however many a chart uses.

### Syntax

The eight syntax roles for code are `callable`, `comment`, `constant`,
`invalid`, `keyword`, `name`, `string`, and `tag`. A full editor theme has more
roles. Helix has fewer because code in documentation only needs to be read.

### Scrollbars

`Scroll Area`, `Chat`, and `Virtual List` color their scrollbars with three
tokens:

- `--helix-scrollbar-thumb-color` is the thumb at rest. It is a mid gray that
  measures at least 3:1 against the page and every surface, in both modes. It
  also clears 3:1 on the rungs inside a Dialog, a Drawer, or a Popover.
- `--helix-scrollbar-thumb-hover-color` is the thumb while the pointer is over
  its scroller. It is `foreground-muted`, a step closer to the text than the
  thumb at rest: darker in light mode and lighter in dark mode.
- `--helix-scrollbar-track-color` is the track behind the thumb. It is
  `transparent`, so the scroller's own ground shows through.

Each of the three reads its own hook first, such as `--scroll-area-thumb-color`
or `--chat-scrollbar-thumb-color`, so you can recolor one scroller and leave
the rest. Set the tokens to recolor all three. Other families that scroll keep
the browser's own scrollbar.

## Why colors use OKLCH

Every Helix color is written in OKLCH. In sRGB, equal numeric steps do not look
equal, which makes ramps and contrast math unreliable. A ramp with even numeric
steps looks uneven. A contrast solver that works in sRGB cannot converge on a
target that it can measure.

OKLCH lets the theme engine *solve* for a palette from your brand color. See
[Deriving a theme](./theming).

## How the engine meets contrast targets

The engine measures each ink against the ground it will be painted on. A brand
color that already meets the target there is used as it is. For any other color,
the engine searches for the lightness that meets the target. A pair that cannot
meet its target, such as one with a color you pinned, is reported in
`violations`.

`foreground` and `foreground-muted` are also written in fields, so the engine
solves them against the well over the page, a card, a raised surface, a dialog,
and a sunken surface. The report names each of these grounds as the well over
its surface, such as `color-well over color-surface`.

Both inks are also written on a row under the pointer, a chosen row, a pressed
row, and a row tinted as selected. The engine solves them against these grounds
as well:

- `hover`, `selected`, and `press` over each of the same five surfaces, such as
  `color-hover over color-surface` and `color-press over color-surface`
- `primary-subtle` and `accent-subtle`, the tints a selected row takes
- the well over each tint, for a field on a selected row

`foreground-subtle` is the one ink the engine does not solve. The range of
readable grays has room for one quiet ink, and `foreground-muted` is that ink, so
`foreground-subtle` stays a fixed step of the stylesheet's gray ramp and its
contrast is not guaranteed. A component paints its quiet text in
`foreground-muted`, and keeps `foreground-subtle` for marks that are not text and
for a mark that recedes.

The engine can solve only the pairs it knows about, so we check two things:

- We compare the engine's surface model with what the components paint.
- We measure every pair on a rendered page.

The second check finds pairs that no single CSS rule states. Only eleven rules
in the library set an ink and a ground together. Another 165 set only an ink,
and 112 set only a ground. The cascade combines them into the rest of the
pairs, and each of those pairs must still be readable.

## Use only semantic tokens

```css
/* Yours to read. */
color: var(--helix-color-foreground-muted);

/* Not yours to read, and the `tokens` rule fails the build on it. */
color: var(--_helix-neutral-500);
```

Primitive tokens such as `--_helix-neutral-500` belong to `helix/styles`. The
underscore marks them as private. Components use only semantic tokens, such as
`--helix-color-primary`. A theme can change what a semantic token resolves to,
and no component depends on which step of which ramp a color comes from.
