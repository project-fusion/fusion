# Maps

Helix draws no tiles. `Map` is the frame around a map a renderer draws, MapLibre
or Leaflet or one of your own: a named region, pan and zoom as buttons, and the
tiles' credit. What a map holds, its places, routes, areas, a live position and
data, are elements you write inside that frame. `@fusion.dev/helix-map` is the
connector between the two, and a style made from your tokens.

## Connecting a renderer

```sh
bun add @fusion.dev/helix @fusion.dev/helix-map maplibre-gl
```

```html
<helix-map label="Our stores">
  <div id="map"></div>
  <helix-map-marker label="Flagship" lat="30.2672" lng="-97.7431"></helix-map-marker>
</helix-map>
```

```js
import '@fusion.dev/helix'
import 'maplibre-gl/dist/maplibre-gl.css'
import { colorsReady, connectMap } from '@fusion.dev/helix-map'
import { followTheme, mapLibreDriver, mapLibreStyle } from '@fusion.dev/helix-map/maplibre'
import maplibregl from 'maplibre-gl'

const frame = document.querySelector('helix-map')
const style = (colors) => mapLibreStyle(colors)
const map = new maplibregl.Map({
  attributionControl: false,
  container: 'map',
  style: style(await colorsReady(frame)),
})

const connection = connectMap(frame, mapLibreDriver(map))
connection.fit({ animate: false })
followTheme(map, frame, style)
```

**`connectMap` does everything that is the same for every renderer.** The
frame's pan and zoom answer through the renderer, over your theme's motion
tokens and at once where the reader asked for less motion. The renderer's own
keyboard handler is turned off and its canvas taken out of the tab order, so a
reader meets one named stop and one set of keys. Its zoom buttons give way to
the frame's, and its credit moves into the frame's `attribution` slot as text
and links. `fit()` frames every place the map holds. `disconnect()` puts back
everything it changed.

**Neither renderer is a dependency.** `mapLibreDriver(map)` takes MapLibre GL
JS 5 and `leafletDriver(map, L)` takes Leaflet 1.9, typed by the calls they make
on it, so you bring whichever version you have. Leaflet draws routes, areas and
points only where you hand it `L`, since the connector imports no renderer.

## A style from your tokens

`mapLibreStyle(colors)` writes a MapLibre style from five tokens read where the
map is: `surface` is the land, and everything else is the land moved toward
something. Water leans toward `info`, parks toward `success`, and the major
roads toward `primary`. The smaller roads, buildings, borders and labels lean
toward `foreground`. One call draws a light map on a light theme and a dark map
on a dark one.

**Every label clears a contrast target** against the land it is haloed in, 4.5
unless you pass `contrast`, so a theme whose ink is already gray still gets
legible place names. `language` picks which name a place is labeled with, and
`tiles` and `glyphs` point the style at your own tile server.

**`followTheme` keeps the map in the theme.** A mode, a class or an inline token
changed on the frame or anything above it, or the reader's light or dark
preference changing, restyles the map by a diff, once a frame, and only where
the style it builds differs. Where your page restyles some other way, such as
replacing a stylesheet, call `update()` on what it returns. `colorsReady` waits
out the frame or two a stylesheet can lag behind the page. Where the tokens
never arrive, it names the one that is missing.

## What a map holds

| Element | What it is |
|---|---|
| `helix-map-marker` | A named place: a button on its point, walked by the keyboard |
| `helix-map-route` | A line the renderer draws under its place names |
| `helix-map-area` | A shape the renderer fills |
| `helix-map-position` | Something moving: a point or an arrow, with a ring as wide as its accuracy |
| `helix-map-points` | Thousands of places as one layer, from GeoJSON |

Each takes `tone`, so its color comes from the same tokens as the rest of the
page and follows the theme with the map.

### Markers

```html
<helix-map-marker label="Texas Capitol" lat="30.2747" lng="-97.7405" shape="ring">
  <span slot="icon">B</span>
</helix-map-marker>
```

A marker is a button named by its `label`, standing on its place. Its name shows
on hover and focus, turning to the other side of the point near the edge of the
view. The `icon` slot holds a letter, a number or an icon, and `shape` sets the
point's form: `ring`, `rounded`, `square`, `diamond`, or `pin` for where a
journey ends. Pressing one sends `helix-activate` from the frame with its `name`,
or its `label` where it has none.

**A place already passed takes `passed`**, which your page sets, since only it
knows where its traveler is. The point turns to the muted ink, and the name says
so too, `passed` by default and `passed-label` to change it.

### Clusters

Put `cluster` on the frame and markers whose points would draw over each other
gather into one, drawn as their count and named by their names. It is asked
again as the view moves, so a cluster comes apart as the map zooms in. Pressing
one zooms in to its places. **Places no zoom can draw apart**, such as two
businesses at one address, fan out around their spot instead, and Escape folds
them back. Connect with `clusterZoom: false` to answer `helix-map-cluster`
yourself, by listing the places in a panel, for example.

### Routes and areas

```html
<helix-map-route
  label="Depot to Mission"
  description="4.2 km"
  points="37.7955,-122.3937 37.7644,-122.427"
  progress="0.4"
></helix-map-route>
```

`points` are `lat,lng` pairs, in the order a marker's own coordinates read. A
route draws the path it is given, straight between each pair, so a path along
roads comes from a routing engine and a page passes its geometry. `progress`
draws the part already traveled quieter, and `variant="dotted"` draws the last
steps off the road network. To move something along a route and dim it behind,
measure with `distancesAlong`, the measure the connector cuts a route with.

### A live position

```html
<helix-map-position
  label="Van 12"
  lat="37.78"
  lng="-122.41"
  heading="90"
  accuracy="30"
  description="Two stops away"
></helix-map-position>
```

Set `lat`, `lng` and `heading` as it moves. It moves in silence: what a screen
reader hears is its `description`, at most once every `announce-every`
milliseconds, so a step is not news and two stops away is. Its heading is drawn
against the view, so it still points the way it is going on a map the renderer
has turned.

### Thousands of points

```html
<helix-map-points label="Food and drink" src="/places.geojson" cluster></helix-map-points>
```

A marker is an element, and the cafés in a city are thousands. A points layer is
one layer the renderer draws, from a GeoJSON file in `src` or data a script sets
as `data`. With `cluster`, MapLibre gathers them into counts, and pressing a
count zooms in to it. Pressing a point sends `helix-activate` from the layer
with its id, or the property `name-key` names. Leaflet draws every point,
unclustered, which suits hundreds but not thousands.

## Keyboard and screen readers

- **The frame is a named region and one tab stop.** The arrow keys pan and
  plus and minus zoom, and its controls are buttons with names.
- **The markers are one tab stop** walked by the arrow keys in the order you
  wrote them, and a marker reached off screen is brought into view.
- **A points layer is one tab stop** over what the renderer drew in view, walked
  top to bottom and along each line. Enter on a count zooms in to it, and the
  focus goes to what then stands nearest.
- **Routes, areas and points are named in a list** inside the frame, by `label`
  and `description`, since a line in a canvas is otherwise nothing to a screen
  reader.
- **A live position speaks its `description` politely**, and never its moves.

## Writing a driver

A driver is an object with `panBy(x, y, motion)` in pixels and
`zoomBy(direction, motion)`, where `motion` is your theme's `{ duration, easing }`
or `null` for a jump. Everything else is optional, and each piece turns on a
feature:

- `project` and `onView`, so markers and positions can stand on their places;
- `show`, to bring a focused marker into view;
- `fit`, for `connection.fit()` and for opening clusters;
- `layers`, to draw routes, areas and points;
- `credits`, to fill the attribution slot;
- `silenceKeyboard`, `focusTarget` and `replaceZoom`, to hand the keyboard and
  the zoom buttons to the frame;
- `bearing`, where your renderer can turn the view.

A driver without `project` leaves its markers hidden rather than drawn in the
corner. `motionOf(element, pace)` reads the same motion for a move your page
makes itself.
